# ADR 0002 — x402: do not accept it in production now; ship it behind a flag

- Status: accepted (E30), 2026-10-25
- Deciders: owner; drafted for review by the E29–E31 implementer
- Scope: whether the hosted gateway (`apps/api`) accepts per-call x402 payments next to the prepaid card wallet
- Related: README.md §7 ("Do not build … our own payment rails or an x402 wallet … decide x402 later"), §11 (payment design); `docs/implementation-plan.md` E9-T2, E30, §7.3, §9; `docs/api/402.md`; research notes *agent_infra_and_protocols.md* §1f, *business_model_gtm_japan.md* (agent-native payments), *monid_and_agent_tool_marketplaces.md* (Monid's x402 rail)

## Decision

**We do not accept x402 in production.** The 402 stays card-only (`accepts: [{ rail: "card" }]`), and prepaid credits through Stripe remain the only way to pay.

**We ship the implementation anyway, behind `X402_ENABLED` (default off)**, so that turning it on is a configuration change, not a project: the 402 gains an `x402` entry, `X-PAYMENT` is verified through a facilitator, replays are refused, the receipt records `funding: "x402"`, and `X-PAYMENT-RESPONSE` is returned (`apps/api/src/billing/x402.ts`, `docs/api/402.md` "The x402 rail", `apps/api/test/x402.test.ts`). The 402 shape has followed x402's `payment-required` field names since E9, so agents that read x402 already read our 402.

## Why not now

### 1. There is not enough real commerce to justify the cost

| Figure (research notes) | Value |
|---|---|
| Cumulative x402 transactions by late April 2026 | ~165M transactions, ~$50M cumulative volume (one source); 50M+ transactions (another). The figures conflict. |
| Transactions by chain, March 2026 | 119M on Base, 35M on Solana |
| **Real commerce volume** | **≈ $28K per day, at ≈ $0.20 per transaction** |
| **Share that is test traffic** | **Artemis estimates about half** |
| Coinbase Agentic.Market (Bazaar) | 165M transactions, $50M volume, 480K+ transacting agents (Coinbase-reported) |

$28K a day across the whole ecosystem is smaller than a single mid-size API business. The research notes' own verdict is "rails ahead of demand": agent wallets paying for APIs are negligible in organic volume, and 402-compatible billing is "cheap insurance, not a growth channel in 2026". Our 402 already is that insurance.

### 2. Per-call settlement does not fit our prices

Our metered prices are sub-cent: ingest is $0.0005 per event, a first-party run $0.002, and the median third-party per-call price in the comparable catalog is $0.0015 (78% at or under $0.01). Reads are free. Monid, the one router that accepts x402, raises any x402 run to a **$0.01 floor** and does not offer metered (per-unit, per-time) endpoints over x402 at all. That floor is the market telling us what one on-chain settlement costs to operate once facilitator, gas, monitoring and reconciliation are counted. At our prices a per-call x402 payment would cost the agent 5–20× the call, or cost us the difference. A prepaid wallet amortizes one card fee over many calls; x402 `exact` settles every call. Until the protocol standardizes a session or deferred-settlement scheme, per-call x402 is structurally wrong for a sub-cent product.

### 3. Governance and backers are strong, so waiting costs little

The Linux Foundation launched the x402 Foundation on 14 July 2026 with 40 founding organizations; Coinbase, Cloudflare and Stripe developed the protocol and Coinbase contributed it to open governance. Members reported include Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, AWS, Google and Shopify; the premier (board-seat) tier reportedly includes Mastercard, Ripple, Shopify, the Solana and Stellar foundations, Stripe and Visa (the Linux Foundation's own release says "initial intent and support"). AWS published a reference implementation in March 2026 and Ramp integrated x402 in August 2026. Stripe integrated x402 for USDC on Base in February 2026 and also runs its fiat Machine Payments Protocol. This is the opposite of a protocol that might disappear: it will still be there when demand arrives, and the flag lets us follow it in a day. Nothing about acting later locks us out; acting now buys almost no volume.

### 4. The compliance load is real for a company selling to agents

We are the merchant of record for metered services sold to agents that may have no human attached at payment time. Accepting a stablecoin per call adds obligations that the card rail delegates to Stripe:

- **Payment-services regulation.** In Japan, USDC is an "electronic payment instrument" under the amended Payment Services Act; handling it for others requires registration. Receiving it as a merchant for our own services, and converting or holding it, needs counsel's confirmation of which activities stay outside the registration and which counterparties (a registered exchange, the facilitator) must do the rest. The same question exists per jurisdiction for US money-transmission rules.
- **Sanctions and AML.** A card payment arrives screened by the card network and Stripe. An x402 payer is a wallet address; we would rely on the facilitator to screen it (OFAC and equivalents), and we must be able to show that we did.
- **Refunds and disputes.** README §11 promises "disputes are an API, not an email" and "failed and empty results cost nothing". On-chain payments are final; a refund is a new outbound transfer to an address, with its own screening. Our implementation avoids most of this by settling only after a successful call, but credits for disputed calls would still be outbound crypto payments.
- **Accounting and tax.** Crypto-asset receipts, valuation at receipt, conversion gains, and consumption tax on cross-border digital services sold to unidentified payers all need a documented treatment before the first live payment.

None of this is impossible; all of it is work we should do once, when the volume pays for it.

## What would change the decision

Turn `X402_ENABLED` on in production when **criterion 1 is met and at least one of 2–4 is met**:

1. **Mandatory — legal and accounting sign-off.** A written opinion that receiving USDC per call for our own services needs no registration we lack (Japan PSA and the US), a facilitator that screens payers against sanctions lists with an audit trail we can retain, and a documented accounting and tax treatment. Without this the flag stays off whatever the demand.
2. **Demand in the market.** Independent data shows real (non-test) x402 commerce of **at least $250K per day, sustained for 60 days** (about 10× the $28K/day in the notes), or test traffic falling below 20% of activity.
3. **Demand from our users.** **At least 3 design partners or paying customers** ask for x402 because their agents carry wallets and cannot use a card top-up, or **5% of agents that receive a 402** in a month present an `X-PAYMENT` header (measurable without turning the rail on: the header is ignored while the flag is off, and the count can be added to the metering log).
4. **Settlement cost fits our prices.** Either the all-in cost of one settlement (facilitator fee + gas + operations) is **at most 1% of our median metered call**, or the x402 Foundation standardizes a session/deferred scheme (pay once, draw down per call) that the facilitator supports — in which case the x402 rail becomes "top up the wallet in USDC", not "pay each call", and the flag's per-call path should be re-scoped accordingly. Stripe settling x402 payments into our existing Stripe balance in fiat (no crypto custody on our side) satisfies this criterion and most of criterion 1.

Re-evaluate every quarter against these numbers, and immediately if Stripe ships fiat settlement of x402 into a Stripe balance. Remove the flagged code if, at the 2027-10 review, none of 2–4 has moved.

## Consequences

- The 402 stays card-only in production; agents that only speak x402 cannot pay us today. That is the cost we accept.
- The flagged path is tested in CI (`apps/api/test/x402.test.ts`) so it does not rot: the 402 entry, replay refusal, facilitator verification, `funding: "x402"` receipts, settlement only after a `2xx`, and caps binding x402-funded calls.
- Known risk if the flag is turned on before criterion 4: a payment that verifies but fails to settle after a successful call leaves that call unpaid (we do not charge the wallet as a fallback). Caps bound the exposure per key; the receipt shows `cost 0` and the log counts `x402_settle_failed`.
- The router's own 402 (`apps/router`, the in-memory metering stub) is unchanged; once the router's `MeteringClient` is wired to apps/api's wallet, the router inherits whichever rails apps/api offers.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Accept x402 now.** Rejected for the reasons above: little real volume, a per-call cost floor above our prices, and compliance work with no revenue behind it.
- **Accept x402 as a USDC top-up rail only (one payment credits the wallet).** Closer to the right shape (one settlement amortized over many calls) but it is a custody and payment-services question first (criterion 1), and x402 is designed for per-request payment; a wallet top-up in USDC is better served by a Stripe crypto on-ramp if one is offered. Revisit under criterion 4.
- **Do nothing until demand appears.** Rejected: the 402 already speaks x402's shape, and a tested, flagged implementation costs little to keep and turns weeks of work into a configuration change.
