## Key Question 1 — Monid profile (founders, funding, launch, business model, payments, listing, catalog, reception, weaknesses)

### Takeaway
Monid (Monid Inc., Delaware C-Corp, San Francisco) is a ~6-month-old, $9.8M-funded "OpenRouter for agent tools": one base URL (`api.monid.ai`), one prepaid wallet (or USDC via x402), three verbs (discover/inspect free, run metered), MIT-licensed connector engine. Its catalog is dominated by web search/scrape/social-data suppliers; no memory/context/state tool is listed. Its marketing says "0% markup" while secondary press reports a 10% platform fee, and an independent August 2026 review found catalog concentration (one supplier = 56% of endpoints), a ranking that surfaced a 26x-more-expensive endpoint first, no published terms/privacy policy, and no per-key spend cap at that time.

### Inferences
- Monid is primarily a *data-acquisition* router (search, scrape, social, enrichment) sold to agent builders doing lead-gen/SEO/market research; its SKILL.md triggers are literally "web scraping, data retrieval, enrichment, social media, product/company/people data, search results, content monitoring" — memory/context is outside its current positioning.
- Revenue model is most plausibly a provider-side or embedded spread (press-reported 10%) plus wallet float, while the agent-facing pitch stays "0% markup" — treat the exact mechanism as unconfirmed.
- The affiliate program paying 20% of referred spend for 6 months implies gross margin on spend is comfortably above 20%, which is inconsistent with literal 0% take unless provider-side discounts fund it (estimate/inference).
- Consistency gaps (tool counts, fee statements, legal pages) suggest a very early company moving fast; a listing partner should expect process to change.

## Key Question 2 — Competitor aggregators / marketplaces for agent tools

### Takeaway
The category has split into (a) metered routers/wallets (Monid, Coinbase Agentic.Market/x402 Bazaar), (b) integration/auth platforms selling to developers on subscription + per-call overage (Composio, Arcade, Pipedream, Zapier MCP, Nango), (c) free discovery registries (official MCP Registry ~17k servers, Glama ~50k, Smithery ~6k — Smithery acquired by Arcade Aug 2026), and (d) platform-owned app directories with review gates (Anthropic directory, OpenAI Apps). Memory services (mem0, Zep, Supermemory) are already listed on Composio and Glama/Smithery, but not on Monid.

### Inferences
- Only two players actually *meter and settle money per tool call across many third-party vendors* with an agent-first wallet: Monid (fiat wallet + x402) and Coinbase's x402 Bazaar/Agentic.Market (crypto-only). Everyone else is either a subscription dev platform or a free index.
- Consolidation is underway (Arcade→Smithery; Stripe→OpenRouter); registries without a payment or auth layer struggle to monetize and become acquisition targets or features.
- Memory services are distributed today as *MCP servers / toolkits inside developer platforms* (Composio, Smithery, Glama) sold on the vendor's own subscription — not as metered per-call tools inside a wallet router.

## Key Question 3 — Economics of an aggregator (OpenRouter analog, take rates, cold start, what agents actually buy)

### Takeaway
OpenRouter proves a thin (~5–5.5%) take on high-velocity usage can reach ~$140–160M annualized revenue and a $7B+ exit, but only on ~$2.5B+ of routed spend from 2.5M users; tool routers have far smaller per-call tickets (Monid median $0.0015) so volume must be enormous. Monid's ranking data shows agents buy commodity web data (search/scrape/social/enrichment) — low-switching-cost, price-compared, substitutable goods — which is exactly where a router adds value; memory/context is stateful, user-bound and sticky, which is why it does not appear and why it is a poor fit for a per-call spot market.

### Cited Findings
- OpenRouter take rate ~5% on inference spend; 8.4T tokens/month; 2.5M users; 400+ models; revenue estimates $50M ARR end-2025 → $140M (July 2026) → $160M (Aug 2026, ~14% MoM); 1,840% YoY growth in 2025; seed $12.5M (a16z, Feb 2025), Series A $28M (Menlo, Apr 2025), Series B $113M at $1.3B (CapitalG, May 2026); Stripe agreed to acquire for >$7B (Aug 2026, unconfirmed by companies) — [Sacra OpenRouter](https://sacra.com/c/openrouter/); [TechCrunch, 2026-05-26](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/26/openrouter-more-than-doubles-valuation-to-1-3b-in-a-year/). Some sources give 5.5% on credit purchases and 8% for a Business tier (Sept 2026) — [usagepricing](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/openrouter); ~$2.5B annualized spend crossing the platform with ~$140M retained (third-party estimate) — [multiples.vc](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/openrouter)
- OpenRouter risks named by analysts: labs going direct or restricting access; "low technical barriers could drive price competition and erode the 5% commission"; open-source routers (LiteLLM) and hyperscaler bundling — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/openrouter/); "systematically eroding its own reason to exist" — [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/L3-yh54B6tLPsnrZE-bl)
- Marketplace take-rate reference points: Apify 20% commission (developer keeps 80% minus compute) — [Apify PPE docs](https://docs.apify.com/actors/publishing/monetize/pay-per-event.md); MCPX "publishers retain 85% via Stripe Connect" — [Stork/MCPX](https://www.stork.ai/en/mcpx); Monid press-reported 10% platform fee — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/monid-7-7m-seed-signals-065006248.html); Notis resells Monid at +20% — [Notis review](https://www.notis.ai/blog/monid-review/); OpenRouter ~5% — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/openrouter/)
- Ticket size: across 1,503 Monid PER_CALL endpoints median $0.0015, 78% ≤ $0.01, only 4 ≥ $1 — [Notis review](https://www.notis.ai/blog/monid-review/)
- What agents buy (Monid, week to 2026-10-20): top 5 tools are all web search/scrape/social-data (TinyFish, TikHub, Context.dev, AnyAPI, Litescrape); lead-gen/enrichment next (Hunter, Ploid, Apollo, ContactOut); gen-AI media (GPT Images, ElevenLabs, MiniMax, Seedance) mid-tail; the only stateful item is Monid's own Simple FS at 5.5K calls — [monid.ai/rankings](https://monid.ai/rankings)
- Cold start evidence: supply concentrated in one anchor supplier (56%) plus long tail; "one anchor supplier plus a long tail" — [Notis review](https://www.notis.ai/blog/monid-review/); Coinbase's Agentic.Market analysts: "adoption could stall if major providers don't integrate, leaving the marketplace as a thin catalog" — [Invezz](https://invezz.com/in/news/2026/04/21/coinbase-backed-x402-launches-agenticmarket-to-power-ai-agent-services/); RapidAPI: 40k APIs yet business declined to an asset sale — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/13/nokia-acquires-rapid-the-api-company-once-valued-at-1b/)
- Monid's own SKILL.md tells agents to prefer the user's existing tools over Monid ("Never route around the user's own tools… Monid runs cost the user money; their existing tools may not") — [monid.ai/SKILL.md](https://monid.ai/SKILL.md)
- Marketplace thesis from a16z Speedrun "14 Big Ideas for 2026": AI-native marketplaces built for the buyer; moat is "pricing data, supplier access, demand aggregation, and workflow ownership" (secondary summary) — [VC Cafe](https://www.vccafe.com/requests-for-startups-summer-2026-edition/); [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/articles/a16z-speedrun-ideas-indie-hackers-2026)

### Inferences
- Unit economics (estimate): at Monid's median $0.0015/call and a 10% fee, 4M total transactions (even if all were paid runs) would be on the order of only hundreds to a few thousand dollars of gross take — the business is a volume bet, consistent with Yahoo's caveat.
- Memory is absent from the router because (1) it is user/tenant-scoped state, not a stateless commodity lookup; (2) its value compounds with continuity, so spot-market switching destroys value; (3) memory vendors monetize via subscription tiers (Mem0 $19–249/mo), which a per-call wallet does not model well; (4) Monid's closed category list has no memory category. A per-call read/write to a memory store *can* be metered (per-unit models exist), but it is unlikely to be discovered by a task-fit ranker optimized for "twitter posts"-style queries.
- Router take rates compress toward ~5% when suppliers can go direct; the sustainable margin lives in wallet/float, enterprise features (budgets, DPA, audit), and demand aggregation — not in the connector code, which Monid has open-sourced.

### Gaps
- No primary disclosure of Monid revenue, paid-run count, or fee %; no public breakdown of free vs. paid among the "4M transactions".
- No published study on agent tool-marketplace cold start specifically; the a16z material is on vertical procurement agents and is secondhand.

## Key Question 4 — Strategic assessment for ShogunAgent: (a) build own aggregator + list inside, (b) list on Monid/registries, (c) both

### Takeaway
Building a Monid-style aggregator is technically cheap (MIT fork) but strategically expensive: it is a volume business in commodity web data, now funded ($9.8M) and consolidating, with a platform-owner-as-seller conflict that Monid itself is criticized for (its own Simple FS is in its rankings). The evidence favors (b) with a twist: list ShogunAgent as a first-class tool on the registries agents actually read (MCP Registry, Anthropic directory, Smithery/Glama, Composio toolkit, and Monid once a memory category exists), ship a Monid-style SKILL.md for agent-native onboarding, and position ShogunAgent as the *memory layer that aggregators lack* — including a possible Japan-specific connector catalog as a wedge rather than a general-purpose "OpenRouter for tools".

### Cited Findings (evidence underpinning the assessment)
- Fork feasibility: Monid's engine, compiler, test harness and connectors are MIT-licensed; "The same artifact runs locally, in CI against fixtures, and on the hosted platform"; 188 replay tests; Deno 2.x/TypeScript — [GitHub README](https://github.com/monid-ai/monid). What is *not* open: the hosted wallet, billing, ranking (`scoreBreakdown` undocumented), health telemetry, OAuth, provider contracts — [Notis review](https://www.notis.ai/blog/monid-review/); [API overview](https://monid.ai/docs/api/overview.md)
- Monid already offers a white-label path: "Proxy — Run your own proxy in front of the Monid API. Control validation, billing, and endpoint access end-to-end"; "Direct use… Users never see Monid" — [Integrations](https://monid.ai/docs/integrations/overview.md). Notis did exactly this (curated allowlist, +20% margin, $1.20 per-call refusal cap) — [Notis review](https://www.notis.ai/blog/monid-review/)
- Conflict of interest precedent: Monid lists its own "Simple FS" in its rankings; PH commenters want usefulness-based rather than usage-based ranking — [monid.ai/rankings](https://monid.ai/rankings); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/monid). Arcade/Smithery coverage warned that one vendor controlling "tool ranking, approval and execution" is a concern — [RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/ja/article/arcade-acquires-smithery-agent-tool-supply-chain). OpenAI's rules forbid descriptions that "steer the model toward or away from other plugins" — [OpenAI guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)
- Distribution leverage of registries: official MCP Registry ~17k servers (July 2026) — [Towards AI](https://pub.towardsai.net/mcp-registries-in-mid-2026-one-upstream-won-2ce541b92036); Anthropic directory listing reaches claude.ai + Cowork + Claude Code in one submission — [Claude Code publish docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/publish.md); Composio already distributes Mem0/Zep/Supermemory toolkits — [Composio Zep](https://composio.dev/toolkits/zep/framework/opencode)
- Japan angle: of 100 major SaaS used by Japanese businesses (April 2026): ~20% official MCP, ~15% third-party MCP, ~45% API-only, ~20% no public API; "残り65%はMCPサーバーが存在しない"; "日本のSaaSはほとんど登録されていない" in the official registry; legal/contract SaaS (CloudSign, GMO Sign, LegalOn) zero MCP; official MCPs named: freee, kintone, Chatwork, Sansan, Money Forward — [Zenn, 2026-04-04](https://zenn.dev/kanseilink/articles/a316f473d8d506). freee's OSS MCP server exposes ~270 APIs (Mar 2026) growing to ~330 (Apr 2026) incl. freee Sign — [Hokkaido Shimbun/freee release](https://www.hokkaido-np.co.jp/article/1360561/); [Cloud Watch](https://cloud.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/2089813.html). Monid's catalog has heavy Chinese social (Douyin/Weibo/Bilibili/Zhihu/Kuaishou) but no Japanese SaaS identified — [Notis review](https://www.notis.ai/blog/monid-review/)
- Stickiness/memory inside an aggregator: Monid's SKILL.md already asks agents to persist a session id, read `Hints`, use health "to break ties", file `dispute`s with the user's intent and all discover queries — i.e., it collects per-task tool-fit feedback centrally — [monid.ai/SKILL.md](https://monid.ai/SKILL.md). Notis (a Monid reseller) markets "Remembers your context" as its own differentiator on top of Monid — [Notis review](https://www.notis.ai/blog/monid-review/)
- Governance requirements enterprise buyers raise against routers: DPA, retention statement, documented rate limits, per-user caps — [Notis review](https://www.notis.ai/blog/monid-review/); OpenAI requires published privacy policy and prohibits reconstructing full chat logs or profiling without disclosure — [OpenAI guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)
- Agent-native onboarding, concretely (Monid SKILL.md v0.1.8 structure): YAML frontmatter `name`, `version`, `description` (trigger conditions + explicit exception "if the user already has a dedicated MCP server, API key, or tool for that specific service, use it"); sections: Setup (version-match rule: "the CLI and skill versions must always be the same"; `npm install -g @monid-ai/cli@latest`; `monid setup --client <agent> --email <if provided>`; "Save the most recent skill from https://monid.ai/SKILL.md to your skill directory"), Authentication (account at app.monid.ai, key at /access/api-keys, `monid keys add -k <key> -l main`), When to Use / When NOT to Use (precedence: explicit user instruction > user's existing tools > Monid), Endpoint Health table (healthy/stable/degraded/outage/unknown), Check the Hints, Commands table, Workflow ("generate session ID → discover → inspect → run → poll → (check balance)"), Example Flows 1–4, Cost & Budget Warning (per-query vs per-call limits), Key Management, Run Statuses (READY…TIMED_OUT, BLOCKED = budget/run-cap control), Polling Best Practices, Feedback, Disputes (required `-r`, `--reason`, `-u` user intent), Troubleshooting, Rules for Agents (14 numbered rules, e.g., "Always inspect before running", "Start with conservative limits", "Surface BLOCKED runs to the user", "never include secrets or the user's private data") — [monid.ai/SKILL.md](https://monid.ai/SKILL.md)

### Inferences (the assessment)
- **Option (a) Build our own general aggregator with ShogunAgent inside — not recommended as the primary strategy.** Reasons grounded above: the connector code is a commodity (MIT, 780 forks already); the hard parts (wallet, provider contracts, ranking telemetry, legal/DPA, fraud) are proprietary and capital-intensive; Monid has $9.8M and 2.5–4M transactions of head start; the category's winners are being absorbed by payments/auth incumbents (Stripe, Arcade); the per-call ticket (~$0.0015) means a fee business needs tens of millions of paid calls to matter; and putting ShogunAgent "inside" a catalog we rank invites the exact ranking-neutrality criticism Monid gets, while OpenAI/Anthropic directories police self-steering.
- **Option (b) List ShogunAgent on registries — recommended core.** Memory services already live on Composio/Smithery/Glama; the official MCP Registry and Anthropic directory are free to enter and reach the agents that actually call tools. Monid is a secondary target: it has no memory category today and its ranker is tuned to data-lookup queries, so expect low discover traffic until Monid adds a category; its per-unit usage models could meter ShogunAgent calls (e.g., per state query) if desired.
- **Option (c) "Both", reframed — recommended if ShogunAgent needs a distribution wedge.** Instead of a general router, run a *narrow, Japan-first connector catalog* (e.g., the 45% of major Japanese SaaS that have APIs but no MCP, legal/e-sign vendors with zero MCP) exposed through ShogunAgent's own MCP/REST/CLI and SKILL.md, with L1/L2/L3 permission tiers as the differentiator (Monid has only workspace-level budget/run caps; Notis had to build per-user caps itself). This can be built on Monid's MIT engine *and/or* proxied onto Monid's hosted catalog via its documented Proxy/Direct-use integration (as Notis did) rather than replicating Monid's supply. The world-model/memory layer is the thing routers structurally lack: "which tool worked for which task for this user/project" is per-user state, exactly ShogunAgent's domain, and Monid's dispute/hints/session-id design shows the data exists but is centralized at the router, not owned by the user.
- Conflict-of-interest mitigation if any catalog is run: publish the ranking formula (Monid's `scoreBreakdown` is undocumented and was criticized), separate "house" tools visibly, allow direct-vendor keys to bypass the wallet (Monid's own SKILL.md rule 2), and publish terms/privacy/retention before launch (Monid lacked them in Aug 2026).
- Japan-specific leverage is real but un-aggregated today: official registries have "almost no" Japanese SaaS; freee/MoneyForward/kintone/Sansan/Chatwork ship MCP but legal/HR/e-sign gaps remain. A Japanese-language SKILL.md + connector set is a plausible wedge with little competition from Monid (whose non-English depth is Chinese social platforms).

### Gaps
- No data on Monid's connector acceptance criteria for non-data tools (e.g., stateful SaaS write actions), nor whether it would accept a memory/context provider; must be asked via support@monid.ai (not done — research only).
- No independent measurement of discover traffic a new, low-usage connector receives on Monid (cold-start on the supply side).
- Japanese enterprise demand for agent tool wallets (vs. direct contracts) not researched; the Zenn survey is a vendor's own study.

## Key Question 5 — Concrete listing requirements for ShogunAgent (Monid, Smithery, Glama, MCP Registry, Anthropic directory, OpenAI Apps)

### Takeaway
Monid listing = a Deno/TypeScript connector PR (`provider.ts` + `endpoint.ts` + zod input schemas + recorded fixtures + offline tests) plus an email to support@monid.ai, hand-reviewed; MCP Registry = `server.json` + `mcp-publisher` with namespace proof and a public streamable-HTTP endpoint; Anthropic directory = validated plugin (`claude plugin validate --strict`) submitted from a paid claude.ai account, MCP server submittable as a connector; Smithery = GitHub repo + `smithery.yaml` or external server exposing `/.well-known/mcp-config`; Glama = automatic GitHub indexing; OpenAI Apps = MCP server with annotated tools, published privacy policy, demo account, no digital-goods sales.

### Cited Findings

**Monid connector (what ShogunAgent would need to ship)**
- Repo layout: `connectors/<provider>/provider.ts` (name, metadata, auth, usage model), `endpoints/<name>/endpoint.ts` (metadata, HTTP request details, input schema, timeouts), shared/per-endpoint schemas, `endpoint.test.ts`, `fixtures/*.json`; optional `resources` for durable owned items — [GitHub README](https://github.com/monid-ai/monid)
- Provider def: flat sections mirroring endpoint (`provider.request.baseUrl`, `provider.usage.consolidate`); endpoint overrides provider overrides config defaults; `meta.docsUrl` and `categories` inherit; omit `auth.credentials` for standard `{apiKey}` shape; `webhooks[slug]` for vendor-account streams — [DEVELOPMENT.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/monid-ai/monid/main/DEVELOPMENT.md)
- Endpoint def: id defaults to `request.path`; four hooks with fixed contracts — `input.toRequest` ({input}→RunInput), `usage.consolidate` ({input,output}→{usage, output?}), `output.fromResponse` ({input,output}→Json), `auth.inject` ({request,params}→request parts); zod input schema at `endpoints/<name>/schema/inputs.ts`, only `z.toJSONSchema`-expressible constraints enforced (`.refine` dropped; put cross-field rules in `notes`); `meta.summary` one line, `description` full capability text, `notes` one operational fact per entry — [DEVELOPMENT.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/monid-ai/monid/main/DEVELOPMENT.md)
- Usage model: `usage.consolidate` required (endpoint or provider level), runs on raw vendor response before `fromResponse`, returns `units` in vendor-native units, `cost` via `utils.money.fromDollars`, `evidence` audit receipts, and strips billing fields from output; presets `presets.usage.perCall()` and `presets.usage.perResult()`; rate-card `period`/`lines`/`reconcileUsage` for resources — [DEVELOPMENT.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/monid-ai/monid/main/DEVELOPMENT.md). README: usage models are flat per call, per returned result, or rate per unit (e.g., per 1,000 characters, per second of video) — [GitHub README](https://github.com/monid-ai/monid)
- Fixtures/tests: `deno task record <id> <scenario>` (headers never captured); `synthetic-` prefix allowed until real keys exist; `deno task test` replays offline; `deno task test:live` skips without credentials; `deno task check`, `lint`, `version:check`; endpoint ids locked in `connectors/ids.lock.json` (`deno task ids:check`); categories from closed list `connectors/categories.ts` — [DEVELOPMENT.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/monid-ai/monid/main/DEVELOPMENT.md)
- Submission: PR + email support@monid.ai (PR link, API docs link, company name); hand review; `AGENT.md` lets coding agents draft connectors; Apify actors have `deno task apify:scaffold` — [GitHub README](https://github.com/monid-ai/monid)
- Implication for ShogunAgent: Monid injects *Monid's* credentials in the transport ("credentials injected inside the transport") — the connector model assumes a vendor-level key held by Monid and metered per call, not per-end-user OAuth; per-user state would require Monid's OAuth integration or a `resources`-style construct — [GitHub README](https://github.com/monid-ai/monid); [Integrations](https://monid.ai/docs/integrations/overview.md)

**Official MCP Registry**
- `mcp-publisher init` generates `server.json`; `login github` grants `io.github.<username>/*`; DNS verification for custom namespaces; `publish` validates package (npm needs `mcpName` in package.json matching server.json); remote servers need a public streamable-http or SSE endpoint; ~10 min indexing — [MCP quickstart](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/registry/quickstart.md); [StackOne](https://www.stackone.com/blog/mcp-registry-how-to-get-listed/); [RoxyAPI](https://roxyapi.com/blogs/mcp-registries-where-to-list-your-server); [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/ai/quickstarts/publish-mcp-registry)

**Anthropic directory / Claude Code plugin**
- Pre-release: permanent kebab-case `name`, `displayName`, `version` strategy, `claude plugin validate --strict`, test install from a local marketplace, fill `description/author/homepage/repository` + README, optional `claude plugin eval` — [Claude Code publish docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/publish.md)
- Own marketplace: `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json` with `{"name","owner":{"name"},"plugins":[{"name","source":"./"}]}`; users run `claude plugin marketplace add <owner/repo>` then `claude plugin install <name>@<marketplace>`; auto-update off by default — [Claude Code publish docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/publish.md)
- Directory: submit at claude.ai/directory/manage; requires paid claude.ai plan (Pro/Max own account; Team/Enterprise Owner or Directory permission); portal applies extra rules beyond CLI; some components Claude Code-only; "Submit your plugin, and your MCP server as a connector"; listed plugins load in Claude Code as `<name>@synced` — [Claude Code publish docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/publish.md); automated review then optional "Anthropic Verified" badge — [claude.com submit](https://www.claude.com/docs/plugins/submit)
- CLI hint mechanism for CLIs/SDKs to recommend their plugin in-session — [Claude Code publish docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/publish.md)

**Smithery (now Arcade)**
- Publish via smithery.ai/new with GitHub; `smithery.yaml` runtime `container` (Dockerfile) or `typescript`; config schema rendered as a connect-time form for API keys; external servers register a public URL exposing `/.well-known/mcp-config`; release types hosted/external/stdio — [Smithery publish API](https://smithery.ai/docs/api-reference/servers/publish-a-server.md); [community guide](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@DynamicEndpoints/etsy-mcp/blob/1ebf1ff4db9fa5bfa57d45b7863c7015a7566f42/SMITHERY_DEPLOYMENT.md)

**Glama**
- Indexes open-source MCP servers automatically from GitHub (37,987 on 2026-06-19) — [Towards AI](https://pub.towardsai.net/8-best-mcp-marketplaces-to-discover-mcp-servers-fb6cb082efcb)

**OpenAI Apps SDK**
- Requirements: clear name (no "MCP"/"Plugin" suffix), example prompts, published privacy policy, identity/business verification in OpenAI Platform dashboard, support contact, tool `readOnlyHint`/`destructiveHint`/`openWorldHint` booleans, transparent auth + demo account with sample data, desktop and mobile testing, no trial/demo apps, each public tool works independently, no steering vs. other plugins; data minimization; no profiling without disclosure; "MCP servers must not reconstruct the full chat log"; commerce only for physical goods via external checkout; digital subscriptions/credits not sellable; no ads — [OpenAI submission guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/app-submission-guidelines)

### Inferences
- ShogunAgent's L1/L2/L3 action tiers map directly onto the MCP annotations OpenAI and Anthropic already consume (`readOnlyHint`, `destructiveHint`) — publishing those annotations is table stakes for all directories.
- For Monid specifically, ShogunAgent fits best as a *per-unit metered* provider (e.g., per state query / per context pack) using `presets.usage.perCall()`, with `notes` documenting that results are user-scoped; but because Monid holds the vendor key, a multi-tenant personal-memory product would need Monid's OAuth path, which is "email support" territory.
- A ShogunAgent SKILL.md should copy Monid's proven skeleton (frontmatter triggers + explicit non-trigger exception, version-lock rule, setup/auth, precedence rules, health/hints semantics, cost warning, statuses, feedback/dispute, numbered agent rules) and add permission-tier semantics (what L2/L3 require before acting).

### Gaps
- Monid: no published SLA for connector review; no public statement on accepting stateful/user-scoped providers; endpoint `timeouts` field and pricing field names beyond `usage.consolidate` not in DEVELOPMENT.md (reference is `openspec/changes/define-endpoint-doc-and-engine/design.md`, not fetched).
- Anthropic directory content rules (claude.com pre-submission checklist) not fetched in full.
- Smithery's current official docs (post-Arcade) not retrieved; guidance above is from community deployment guides.
