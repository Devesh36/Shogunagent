# Business models, GTM, pricing, funding and market sizing for agent-facing memory/context infrastructure — with a Japan-founder lens (state as of 2026-10-25)

Scope note: Pricing tables below were pulled directly from vendor pricing pages on 2026-10-25 unless marked "third-party". Funding figures come from press releases, TechCrunch, Sacra, CB Insights, Dealroom, etc.; where sources conflict, both figures are given. "Est." = estimate or my own arithmetic, not a vendor-stated figure.

## KQ1. Pricing norms and unit economics for memory/context APIs and adjacent agent infra

### Takeaway
The 2026 norm for memory APIs is "free tier + $19–20 developer tier + $100–400 scale tier + custom enterprise", metered on a single abstract unit (tokens, credits, add/retrieve requests, or active agents) rather than per seat; compliance (SOC 2, HIPAA BAA, self-host/BYOC, zero-data-retention) is consistently gated to the $399+/enterprise tiers and is the main price lever. No memory vendor discloses gross margin; third-party cost models agree that LLM extraction on write (not embedding or storage) dominates COGS.

**COGS and margin evidence (no memory vendor discloses margins)**

- Embedding cost in a production memory system ≈ $0.00002 per 1K tokens (~$200 for 10 billion tokens); storage and vector search "cheap"; cost is driven by reasoning steps: extraction of facts per turn, an LLM "dedup curator" over clusters, and read-time relevance passes. — [memoryplugin field guide: cost & latency](https://www.memoryplugin.com/wiki/cost-and-latency.html)
- Third-party estimate: a 100-turn conversation at GPT-4o pricing costs ~$0.30–$0.80 in memory-management LLM calls (extraction). — [instagit Mem0 Q&A (third-party, unaudited)](https://instagit.com/mem0ai/mem0/what-are-the-token-usage-and-cost-implications-of-using-mem0-at-scale)
- GraphMem cost calculator: extraction ≈ $2,000 per million documents vs ≈ $130 for OpenAI embeddings (≈15:1 extraction:embedding ratio). — [GraphMem production cost docs](https://graphmem.readthedocs.io/en/latest/production/cost/)
- Mem0's own claim: compressed memory snippets cut prompt tokens by up to 80% vs raw history (company claim via Sacra). — [Sacra Mem0 profile](https://sacra.com/c/mem0/)
- Benchmark for API-business gross margins: analysis of OpenAI's API estimated ~75% gross margin in June 2024 falling to ~55% at August-2024 pricing because prices were cut faster than costs. — [FutureSearch OpenAI API profit analysis](https://futuresearch.ai/openai-api-profit)
- Hyperscaler alternative that caps the price umbrella: AWS Bedrock AgentCore, Microsoft Foundry Agent Service memory, Google Vertex named as competitive risk to independent memory vendors. — [TGVP report, 2026-04-29](https://www.tgvp.vc/releases/tgvp-report-ai-agent-infrastructure-in-2026)

### Inferences
- **Implied unit prices (my arithmetic from the tables above):** Mem0 Starter ≈ $0.00038 per add ($19/50K) and Pro ≈ $0.0005 per add ($249/500K); Zep Flex ≈ $0.0025 per credit ($2.50 per 1K 350-byte episodes) with retrieval free; supermemory $0.005 per 1K unique tokens ingested, i.e. a 700-byte (~175-token) message costs ≈ $0.0009 before dedup; Letta charges a platform fee per *active agent* ($0.10/mo) rather than per memory. The market has not converged on a unit — tokens, bytes, operations and agents are all in use — which means a new entrant can define its own unit (e.g., per "tracked commitment/open loop" or per "world-model refresh") without being off-norm.
- **Price anchoring:** the $19–20 developer tier appears in supermemory, Mem0, Letta, Pinecone (Builder), Browserbase (Developer) and Composio ($29). A $19–20 Pro tier is effectively mandatory for credibility with individual developers; the real revenue tiers are $100–400 (supermemory Max/Scale, Zep Flex/Flex Plus, Mem0 Pro) and enterprise minimums ($500/mo Pinecone, $3,000/mo E2B).
- **Compliance is the monetization lever, not capability:** SOC 2, HIPAA BAA, BYOC/self-host, ZDR and audit logs are gated at Scale/Enterprise by every vendor (supermemory $399+, Zep Enterprise, Mem0 Enterprise, Exa Enterprise, Composio add-ons). A local-first vendor (ShogunAgent) whose *default* is "data never leaves the device" inverts this: it can offer the compliance posture at the low tier and charge for sync, multi-device and team features instead.
- **COGS structure (est.):** if extraction ≈ 15× embedding cost and roughly $0.002–0.008 per written turn at frontier-model prices, Mem0's ~$0.0004–0.0005/add list price implies either heavy use of small/cheap extraction models, batching, or near-zero or negative margin on the Starter tier (likely loss-leader). supermemory's "unique tokens only" and Zep's "retrieval is free" are both margin-protection designs: charge where the LLM cost is (ingest), give away where cost is near zero (vector lookups). ShogunAgent's local-first inference on the user's Mac shifts the dominant COGS (extraction) onto the customer's hardware, which is a structural gross-margin advantage if the hosted layer is thin.
- A realistic hosted gross margin for a memory API, by analogy to the 55–75% OpenAI API range and the extraction-dominated cost base, is likely 50–70% at scale (est.), lower in the free/developer tiers.

### Gaps
- No memory vendor (supermemory, Mem0, Zep, Letta, Cognee, Honcho) publishes gross margin, COGS, or revenue; Sacra explicitly lists Mem0 revenue and valuation as "not disclosed".
- Mem0's usage-based/overage rates and Zep's annual prices are not published.
- Letta's two pricing pages conflict (20 vs unlimited agents on Pro); treat the docs page as the API-facing truth.
- Exa does not list Websets or Research endpoint prices on the main pricing page.

## KQ2. Open-source vs hosted strategies, licenses, and whether OSS drove adoption → revenue

### Takeaway
Every notable memory startup except Zep ships a permissive OSS core (Mem0 Apache-2.0, Letta Apache-2.0, Graphiti Apache-2.0, Cognee Apache-2.0, supermemory MIT, Hindsight MIT) with Honcho the lone AGPL-3.0 holdout; Zep retired its self-hosted Community Edition and sells cloud/BYOC only. Stars clearly drive distribution (Mem0 41K→~65K stars, 14M downloads, 5.3× API-call growth in two quarters, AWS exclusive) but there is no public evidence in this category of stars converting to disclosed revenue — the only hard "stars→ARR" datapoint is adjacent (Firecrawl: 8-figure ARR in year one).

### Cited Findings
- **Mem0**: core library Apache-2.0, self-host free; hosted tiers "sell convenience, analytics, and support rather than the capability itself." — [rywalker Mem0 research (third-party)](https://rywalker.com/research/mem0). Press release 2025-10-28: 41,000 GitHub stars, 14 million downloads, API calls grew from 35M (Q1 2025) to 186M (Q3 2025), "thousands of teams" incl. Fortune 500, AWS chose Mem0 as "exclusive memory provider for its Agent SDK" (Strands), native in CrewAI, Flowise, Langflow, "three lines of code". — [Mem0 $24M press release, 2025-10-28](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mem0-raises-24m-series-a-to-build-memory-layer-for-ai-agents-302597157.html). Third-party counts: 65,589 stars as of 2026-09-18. — [GeniOS blog (third-party)](https://thegenios.com/blog/explosive-growth-open-source-memory-layers/)
- **supermemory**: GitHub repo MIT license, 31.4k stars (read 2026-10-25); "Supermemory local" runs the same API at localhost:6767 — "Same API as the platform — prototype locally, ship on the hosted platform by changing baseURL"; URL ingestion still depends on a hosted reader service; client plugins open source. — [supermemoryai/supermemory on GitHub](https://github.com/supermemoryai/supermemory). (Note: an older supermemory docs page described self-hosting as "noncommercial" — outdated vs the MIT repo. — [docs.supermemory.ai pricing (stale)](https://docs.supermemory.ai/essentials/pricing))
- **Letta** (ex-MemGPT): Apache-2.0 per multiple comparison sites. — [vectorize Letta alternatives (third-party)](https://vectorize.io/articles/letta-alternatives)
- **Zep / Graphiti**: Graphiti Apache-2.0; Zep Community Edition deprecated; Zep Cloud is closed with BYOC for enterprise. — [Zep FAQ](https://help.getzep.com/faq)
- **Cognee**: Apache-2.0 (one site labels the business "open core"). — [vectorize Zep vs Cognee (third-party)](https://vectorize.io/articles/zep-vs-cognee)
- **Honcho (Plastic Labs)**: "Honcho is licensed under the AGPL-3.0 License", 7.8k stars, managed service at api.honcho.dev (read 2026-10-25). — [plastic-labs/honcho on GitHub](https://github.com/plastic-labs/honcho)
- **Hindsight (Vectorize)**: MIT, with managed cloud. — [get-hermes.ai memory providers guide (third-party)](https://get-hermes.ai/memory/)
- **Adjacent evidence of OSS→revenue**: Firecrawl job listings state "We hit 8 figures in ARR in year one and more than doubled it in year two" and cite 147k–180k GitHub stars ("top 50 repositories of all time"). Self-reported, undated. — [Firecrawl job posting](https://startup.jobs/senior-growth-engineer-firecrawl-8121216)
- Firecrawl financing: Series A $14.5M (2025-07-11, CB Insights) vs $20.7M led by Nexus + YC (Dealroom); Series B $75M led by Smash Capital; SEC Form D showing $82.06M preferred. — [Dealroom Firecrawl Series B](https://dealroom.co/news/155341-firecrawl-raises-75m-series-b-to-build-a-knowledge-library-for-ai-agents/); [RuntimeWire Form D scoop](https://runtimewire.com/article/scoop-firecrawl-raises-82m-after-its-14-5m-series-a-warm-up)
- Benchmark credibility is contested: "multiple independent researchers have documented inability to reproduce published results (EverMemOS #73, Mem0 #3944, Zep scoring discrepancy)". — [atlan agent-memory frameworks 2026 (third-party)](https://atlan.com/know/best-ai-agent-memory-frameworks-2026/)
- TGVP (2026-04-29) argues connector catalogs and framework wrappers "can be replaced easily and commoditize", while stateful memory accumulates switching costs; MCP has "97M+ monthly SDK downloads" (undated). — [TGVP report](https://www.tgvp.vc/releases/tgvp-report-ai-agent-infrastructure-in-2026)
- Sacra lists Mem0's key risks as "model commoditization, vector database competition, and the difficulty of moving into enterprise sales from a developer-focused go-to-market." — [Sacra Mem0](https://sacra.com/c/mem0/)

### Inferences
- The category default is **permissive OSS core (Apache-2.0/MIT) + hosted cloud + enterprise BYOC**. BSL/fair-source is absent from this category in 2026 (none of the vendors surveyed use it); AGPL is used only by Honcho. For a Rust local-first product, MIT/Apache-2.0 for the client/engine and a closed hosted sync/team layer mirrors supermemory's "same API locally and hosted, switch baseURL" pattern, which is the clearest model of OSS-as-funnel in this space.
- Zep's retreat from self-hosting shows the opposite path works too when the moat is graph quality and ops burden; but Zep is also the least-funded (see KQ4) and most dependent on enterprise BYOC deals.
- Stars correlate with distribution (downloads, integrations, AWS partnership) but the only revenue proof in adjacent dev-infra (Firecrawl) came from a *consumption-metered* API, not from support/enterprise licensing. Expect OSS to buy integrations and credibility, and a metered cloud to monetize.
- "Local by default, same API hosted" is a differentiator vs Mem0/Zep (hosted-first) and roughly parity with supermemory (which has a local mode but cloud-first UX). ShogunAgent's point of difference must therefore come from the data model (state of work), not from "runs locally" alone.

### Gaps
- No disclosed revenue, ARR, or conversion-rate (OSS→paid) data for any memory startup; Letta is listed as "$1.4M est. ARR" by Latka but that site also wrongly calls Letta "bootstrapped", so I do not rely on it.
- Could not verify Mem0's "exclusive memory provider for AWS Agent SDK" from an AWS primary source.

## KQ3. Distribution channels that work in 2026 for agent-facing services (incl. agent-native distribution)

### Takeaway
In 2026 the proven channels for agent infra are (a) being a one-line integration inside the frameworks/SDKs developers already use (Mem0 in CrewAI/LangGraph/Strands; supermemory as MCP server + Claude Code "Hermes" plugin), (b) GitHub-repo-as-marketplace for Claude Code plugins plus Anthropic's curated directory, (c) the MCP registry (still "preview" since Sep 2025) and third-party directories, (d) OpenAI's plugin directory (submissions open since 2025-12-17, monetization still undefined), and (e) seed-stage credibility from YC + named angels. Agent-native payments (x402, ACP, Skyfire) have institutional backing but tiny organic volume (~$28K/day real commerce), so they are a 2027 bet, not a 2026 channel.

### Cited Findings
- **Claude Code plugin marketplaces (official docs, read 2026-10-25):** a marketplace is "a directory or repository with a `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json` file"; users register it with `claude plugin marketplace add <owner>/<repo>` and install `name@marketplace`; sources can be relative path, GitHub repo, git-subdir, URL, zip archive, npm, or command; "Offering a plugin to everyone: submit it to Anthropic's directory"; names like `claude-plugins-official` are reserved; admins can require a marketplace on every machine. — [Claude Code: Create a marketplace](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-marketplaces)

## KQ6. Positioning evidence: local-first / zero-retention / BYO-storage; "state of work" vs generic memory; multi-agent shared memory; agent passports

### Takeaway
Zero-data-retention and in-customer-cloud deployment are now explicit, priced enterprise features across agent infra (Exa ZDR enterprise-only; Composio ZDR +$0.0001/call; supermemory air-gapped; Zep BYOC; Pinecone BYOC) and the Anthropic 30-day retention controversy showed buyer pushback is real; analysts (a16z, Deep Feed, Futurum) converge on "customer-specific business context > generic personalization" and "agent-neutral, permission-aware shared context is becoming table stakes". Direct market evidence for a "state of work" (commitments/open loops) product and for "agent passports" is absent — the adjacent signals are strong but no vendor has validated that exact category.

### Cited Findings
- **ZDR as priced feature:** Exa — Zero Data Retention only on Enterprise; Composio — ZDR add-on +$0.0001/call on Pro, included on Enterprise, plus customer-managed KMS keys; supermemory — Scale ($399) "self-hosted option", Enterprise "air-gapped self-hosting or dedicated managed instance"; Zep — Enterprise BYOK / BYOC in your VPC; Pinecone — BYOC plan; E2B — BYOC on Enterprise; Browserbase — data retention 7 days free / 30 days paid. — [Exa pricing](https://exa.ai/pricing); [Composio pricing](https://composio.dev/pricing); [supermemory pricing](https://supermemory.ai/pricing.md); [Zep pricing](https://www.getzep.com/pricing); [Pinecone pricing](https://www.pinecone.io/pricing/); [E2B pricing](https://e2b.dev/pricing); [Browserbase pricing](https://www.browserbase.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- "Local-first / zero-retention / BYO-storage" is validated as *willingness-to-pay* (vendors charge for it) and as *buyer pressure* (Anthropic retention episode), but it is table-stakes at the enterprise tier rather than a standalone product — it will not differentiate against supermemory Enterprise or Zep BYOC unless it is the *default* for every tier, which a Rust-on-device architecture allows and hosted vendors cannot cheaply match.
- The strongest positioning evidence points to **org-/person-specific operational context ("tribal knowledge", commitments, open loops, who-owes-whom) that is continuously inferred and kept current** — exactly a16z's "living corpus" and Deep Feed's "customer-specific knowledge" — not to another personalization-memory API. "State of work" should be marketed as a *typed* context graph (people, projects, commitments, open loops) with provenance, closer to Jedify/Interloom than to Mem0.
- Multi-agent shared memory is a credible 2027 RFP item; the design requirement analysts name is "permission-aware, agent-neutral, exposed via MCP" — ShogunAI already exposes via MCP/REST/CLI, so the gap is permissioning/audit, not transport.
- "Agent passports" is not an established category term; the adjacent demand is **agent identity (KYA, non-human identity governance)** and **portable agent state** (Letta `.af`). A "passport" that bundles identity + permissions + portable state could be positioned at that intersection but has no direct market validation yet.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifying enterprise demand for local-first or ZDR (searched; only vendor/consultancy blogs found).
- No product or funding evidence for a dedicated "state of work / commitments / open loops" API; the only adjacent products are enterprise-memory/context-graph startups (Interloom, Jedify) and meeting/CoS assistants not surfaced by searches.
- No source uses the term "agent passport"; the Futurum piece is LLM-generated and lacks survey figures.
- Rakuten "97% fewer first-pass errors" is a third-party relay of an Anthropic claim; unverified.
